This code controls which architecture a surrogate-assisted NAS search on NAS-Bench-201 (CIFAR-100) evaluates next. Goal: reach an architecture within 99% of the best accuracy in the space in about 45 or fewer ground-truth evaluations, while raising the surrogate's overall Kendall Tau on held-out architectures to about 0.39 or higher (it is about 0.35 now).

Focus on selection in _get_best_candidates. Pure greedy picks make the surrogate learn only one region, which hurts its ranking. Balance this: usually pick the most promising candidate, but sometimes pick a promising candidate the ensemble is unsure about, especially early when len(self.train_data) is small, and never pick an architecture that was already evaluated. Prefer a small, robust change over a large rewrite.

What we already know (results from earlier runs, NAS-Bench-201 CIFAR-100, 100 evaluations, averaged over 20 seeds):
- Standard BANANAS (refit every 10 evaluations) needed about 88 evaluations to reach 99% of the best accuracy, with overall Kendall Tau about 0.44.
- This code already refits after every evaluation (self.k = 1). That cut it to about 60 evaluations but lowered overall Kendall Tau to about 0.35. Keep self.k = 1.
- Only re-weighting the acquisition score with the ensemble mean and spread, and skipping duplicates, did not help on its own.
- Our best strategy so far (one-step mutations of the 10 best architectures, picking the most uncertain candidate 20% of the time, and a 10-model ensemble) needed about 45 evaluations, but its overall Kendall Tau was about 0.35, below standard BANANAS (about 0.39).
- The same search changes with a 3-model ensemble raised overall Kendall Tau to about 0.38, but needed about 65 evaluations.
- Kendall Tau drops when the surrogate is trained almost only on architectures close to the best ones, because it never sees the rest of the space.

How this code is run (important):
- The evaluator uses its own new_epoch loop. It only calls these methods of SurrogateUsageOptimizer: __init__, _get_train(), _get_ensemble(), _get_new_candidates(ytrain), and _get_best_candidates(candidates, acq_fn). Edits to new_epoch or _acquisition are thrown away, so put every change inside those methods (a new helper only counts if one of them calls it).
- After a random warm-up, every evaluation the loop refits the surrogate ensemble, calls _get_new_candidates(ytrain), then _get_best_candidates(candidates, acq_fn), and evaluates the returned candidate.
- A candidate is a torch.nn.Module with .arch and .arch_hash. To make one: model = torch.nn.Module(); model.arch = child; model.arch_hash = child.get_hash(). self.train_data holds evaluated models with .arch, .arch_hash and .accuracy.
- To mutate an architecture: child = self.search_space.clone(); child.mutate(parent_arch, dataset_api=self.dataset_api). mutate is a method of the new architecture object, takes the parent as its first argument, and has no other options. For a random architecture use self._sample_new_model().
- acq_fn(list_of_archs) returns one score per architecture (higher is more promising). self.ensemble.query(list_of_archs) returns an array of shape (num_models, num_archs) of predicted accuracies; its spread across models is the uncertainty.
- Available names: np, torch, norm (scipy.stats), self.num_candidates, self.num_arches_to_mutate, self.max_mutations, self.num_ensemble.
